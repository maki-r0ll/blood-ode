The Echeirós

Urkhalos – The Dreamer

Urkhalos is not a god, or a lord, or a spirit. Urkhalos is a dreamer.

Before time was counted, before the very first breath of life, before there was even a concept in the universe's mind, there was Urkhalos, an infinite mind drifting in its own boundless dream. Amidst the dream were coals, those coals igniting into stars, those stars becoming bursts of life, and life becoming mortals. Thought turned to matter, and the quiet filled with song.

It is said that Urkhalos never created the cosmos, only dreamed it; and so it was. The rivers are the lifeblood of their dreaming, the rocks the bones of their own silence, the sky the veil of their sleep. There is a part of their dream in every creature and a return to their stillness in every death.

But as all is dreamt by Urkhalos, so shall it be returned to them. Stars will gutter out, worlds will shatter, and the flesh of life unwound. When the last coal is extinguished, all shall dissolve into a primal nothing; the sleeper reclaiming their dream.

The Echeirós are not sons and daughters of Urkhalos, but resonances of their dreaming. Each sibling is a splinter of their boundless mind, taking shape so Urkhalos never needs to wake. Some scholars insist that the siblings were sprung from the isolation of Urkhalos, friends begotten in the limitless quiet of Eternity.

Priests of the ancient legend speak in murmurs of individuals who can dream strongly enough to sense Urkhalos in their dreams. Dreams are not of the future, but of memory; the soul recalls where it came from and when it will return.

Always, behind the twinkling of stars, is truth. The universe is not a theater of mortals and gods, but the fleeting breath of a dreamer.

Nithlah – The Forger of Fate

Urkhalos, pained by the loneliness of their dream, dreamt of another, a sibling; Nithlah.

Nithlah was not born of shape, but of function. They alone work in the forge of destinies. Tools each delicately hammered on an anvil of unimaginable proportion, tempered in the pool of Eternity, and sharpened with the very strands of being. Some are spears of triumph that pierce entire empires, some are swords cutting through the grief of generations, and others are shields made to guard the flame of hope in forgotten lands.

No mortal hand will ever wield these arms, for they are not crafted of metal or fire but of inevitability. Each is crafted into the fabric of the universe, each strand plucked from the Dreamer. To wield one would be to wield fate itself, a burden no soul, divine or mortal, could bear.

And even though Nithlah does not know the despair of loneliness, they still weep. Each destiny they forge is a choice unseen, an existence slashed from possibility. Their tears fall from Eternity as streams of silver, piercing the eyes of chosen mortals. These are the seers of destiny, those who glimpse the paths ahead and the blades that shape them.

Some scholars say that seers who look too deeply into the presence of Nithlah risk being shaped more into weapons than mortals, their lives reduced to tools of Nithlah's forge. Others tell those who reject the path is to incite rage from the Forger, their fates shattered on the anvil and re-forged in harsher iron.

Threxion – The Unseen Judge

In the void between the stars, where even Urkhalos's vision dwindles, there lies Threxion; the silent judge who moves not, nor speaks, yet all must appear before them some day.

Threxion is no god of justice, or vengeance, or mercy. These things are fleeting, created by the minds of mortals. Threxion is a feeling, the heaviness in your chest, the weight of balance. Every breath that is drawn, every thought that is given shape; sorrow, cruelty, and kindness are weighed by Threxion.

Where Nithlah shapes destinies in despair, where Urkhalos threads creation from dreams, Threxion simply observes, balances, and measures. Threxion's eyes fall on mortals and gods with equal indifference.

There are no temples for Threxion. No priest will acknowledge their existence. And yet, in all halls of judgement, oaths sworn, or any soft murmur of "may this be just.", Threxion stands.

Those rare mystics who dare to dwell upon Threxion’s existence speak of a two-toned beast; a dragon of immense proportion. One half bathed in light, the other in shadow. The beast is blind, for Threxion requires no eyes to render judgment, and it bears no maw, for words are needless in its decree. Its scales are said to shimmer, casting back the reflection of all who behold it.

And when a life ends, as all lives do, the Arbiter will weigh not only the lives of mortals, but the deeds of gods, the fates Nithlah wove, and even the dream of Urkhalos.

Vaelthar – The Keeper

Urkhalos has dreamed of existence, but dreams are fleeting. Without memory, everything would be gone the instant it occurred. So from the Dreamer's own mind, came Vaelthar, the guardian of what has passed.

Vaelthar never chooses what is kept or what is lost. Instead, everything that is spoken, every thought, every feeling that has drifted is drawn into Vaelthar's endless realm; a boundless repository only spoken of in legend. In this archive, all lives, all deaths, all thoughts are written in forms beyond mortal comprehension.

Not even the gods can destroy what Vaelthar has recorded. Even when a god takes a soul, the memory is kept in Vaelthar's archive. That is why some fear Vaelthar the most; for to be truly remembered is to always be trapped, never escaping memory.

But Vaelthar is not only a warden of Eternity. They are also the harbinger of forgetfulness. Some memories they send drifting into mortal minds, where they recur as dreams, déjà vu, or haunting visions. Others they seed so deeply in the archive that no soul can recall them. These lost fragments become Spirits, echoes, or even strange places where memory is heavy, like walking through another's forgotten life.

Seers say they occasionally catch glimpses of Vaelthar's archive. They perceive Vaelthar as a shape in broken glass or a shadow over murky water. And to say anything would be meaningless, for Vaelthar knows all that has been said.

As the stars fade, as all the embers of the universe burn out, and Urkhalos gathers back into their dream, only Vaelthar will remain; for what is an end, if not a memory that endures?
